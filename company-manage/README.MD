# Company Manage

## 1. Organizational Structure and Authority Boundaries

The company adopts a minimalist, three-tier flat structure, strictly limiting management layers to ensure clear accountability and frontline execution capability.

### Three-Tier Structure

Boss, Managers, and Employees.

### Strict Hierarchy Constraint

Only a single layer of management is permitted; managers managing other managers is strictly prohibited.

### Authority Boundaries

* Boss: Holds final decision-making and approval authority, including hiring, salary adjustments, and bonus distribution.

* Managers: Responsible for project scheduling, technical solution design, and core implementation. Each team has three managers. They must participate in hands-on frontline development (managers leading a team may be assigned a moderately reduced workload). Managers have no voting rights.

* Employees: Responsible for executing development tasks, holding the right to elect project managers and the obligation to accept performance evaluations.

## 2. Task Lifecycle

### Proposal Formulation and Timelines

When the company receives a new development task, the three managers first hold a joint meeting to discuss requirements, then independently formulate their own development proposals and project timelines.<br/>

### High-Priority Tasks

If the task is classified as High Priority, the three managers must report to the Boss, who evaluates each manager's proposal and allocates distinct dedicated bonuses accordingly.<br/>

### Proposal Presentation and Democratic Election

The three managers present their respective proposals, timelines, and corresponding task bonuses to all employees.<br/>

All employees vote to elect the responsible manager for that specific task. The manager with the highest vote leads all employees to execute the task together. in the event of a tie, the system makes a determination.<br/>

### Concurrent Task Mode

When concurrent tasks arise, after employees vote to elect a manager, the elected manager independently selects a subset of employees to form a dedicated execution squad.<br/>

## 3. Task Scoring

### Task Point Valuation

Before discussing the task with employees, the three managers independently score the task. The sum of the three managers' scores constitutes the final Task Score for that task.<br/>

### Employee Performance Evaluation

The manager who leads the team to complete the task receives 100% of the Task Score.<br/>

Upon task completion, the leading manager must rate the participating team members (Positive / No Rating / Negative).<br/>

* Positive: 100% of the task score.

* No Rating: 50% of the task score.

* Negative: 25% of the task score.

## 4. Bonuses

### Per-Task Bonus

The bonus is distributed directly upon task completion. The total bonus pool is fixed, and the allocation ratio is determined by each member's individual score for that task.<br/>

### Annual Bonus

The total bonus pool is fixed, and the allocation ratio is determined by each member's total accumulated score for the year.<br/>

## 5. Salary Increase

When the company decides to award pay raises, it may award either employees or managers with higher performance scores.<br/>

## 6. Promotion

When a role opens up, the company may fill it via external hiring or internal promotion of top performers. For internal promotions, candidates may be selected from among employees or managers with higher performance scores.<br/>

## 7. Annual Dismissal and Cyclical Iteration

At the end of each year, the manager with the lowest performance score in each team is mandatorily eliminated.<br/>

Following elimination, the company recruits a new manager to fill the vacancy, ensuring sustained vitality and competitive pressure within the management layer.<br/>
