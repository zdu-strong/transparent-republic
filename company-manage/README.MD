# Company Management

## Example - mobile app development


The company has a mobile app team responsible for developing mobile apps.<br/>

The mobile app team is equipped with three managers.<br/>

Every time a development task is received, the three managers first hold a meeting together to discuss the requirements.<br/>

Then, they meet with the employees to explain the scope and details of the task, and the employees vote to select the manager for this specific task.<br/>

The manager with the most votes will manage the development task, and all employees will work with them. If there is a tie in votes, the system will determine the manager.<br/>

If a concurrent task arises, all employees vote to select the manager responsible for it, and the elected manager then selects a portion of the employees to execute the task.<br/>

## Example - Task Score

Before discussing each task with the employees, the three managers score the task individually. The sum of the scores evaluated by the three managers will serve as the final score for the task.<br/>

Managers who lead their team to complete the task receive 100% of the task score.<br/>

After each task is completed, the manager who led the task rates the participating team members as "Positive," "Negative," or "No Rating."<br/>

* Positive: 100% of the task score.

* No Rating: 50% of the task score.

* Negative: 25% of the task score.

## Example - Salary Increase

When the company decides to award pay raises, it may award either employees or managers with higher performance scores.<br/>

## Example - Promotion

When a role opens up, the company may fill it via external hiring or internal promotion of top performers. For internal promotions, candidates may be selected from among employees or managers with higher performance scores.<br/>

## Example - Project Timeline and Bonus Allocation

Whenever a development task is received, the three managers first meet together to discuss the requirements and determine their respective proposals and timelines.<br/>

Then, if the task is of high priority, the three managers consult with the boss, who assigns different bonuses to each of their proposals.<br/>

The three managers present their plans and corresponding task bonuses to the employees, who then vote to select the manager to execute the task.<br/>

## Example - Organizational structure.

The company operates on a three-tier structure: Boss, Managers, and Employees.<br/>

There is only one layer of management. Do not have managers managing managers. Managers must do the same work as their employees.
<br/>

Hiring requires the Boss's approval, and salaries and bonuses also require the Boss's approval.<br/>

Project timelines are decided by the Managers.<br/>

Managers must also do hands-on work, and the manager leading the team can assign themselves a smaller workload.<br/>

The manager leading the task execution receives 100% of the task score. The two assisting managers participating in the task receive a fixed 50% of the task score.<br/>
