# Citizen Assessment

No one is born a citizen. Even the children of citizens are regarded as residents at birth, and they must complete the required process before they can ultimately become citizens.

## Types of Legal Status

### 1. Citizen
Entitled to a basic living allowance, sovereign dividends, voting rights, and the right to run for office.<br/>
Holds nationality and a passport. Dual or multiple citizenship is not permitted.<br/>

### 2. Immigrant
Entitled to a basic living allowance.<br/>
Holds nationality and a passport. Dual or multiple citizenship is not permitted.<br/>

### 3. Resident

Resident can live there, buy land, and do business.

### 4. Anonymous

Anonymous can buy a receipt printer and start doing business.<br/>
An official shall assist the person in registering as a resident. If the person repeatedly refuses to register, they may be prosecuted.<br/>

## Citizenship Assessment Requirements

1. Must be at least 18 years old.<br/>
2. Must have held immigrant status for at least 6,574 days.<br/>
3. Must have received the Basic Living Security Grant for at least 6,574 days.<br/>
4. Must be able to speak the country’s language.<br/>
5. Must understand the political system:<br/>
A 20-question multiple-choice test, with a passing score of 60%.<br/>

## Quantity restriction

There is no limit on the number of residents or citizens.<br/>

Resources are limited, so the number of residents who can become immigrants each day is limited and determined by random selection.<br/>

There is no limit on the number of residents under one year old who may obtain immigrant status.<br/>

However, human conscience must not grow cold. A Special Rescue Department shall therefore be established to help people whose suffering receives exceptional media attention.<br/>

## Entry Risks

For residents with a criminal risk profile and anonymous individuals, the court may, upon entry—based on clear legal grounds including country of origin—issue a collective ruling sentencing them to serve 3 months in a low-security areas of prison. This measures serves to alleviate safety concerns among citizens while maintaining open borders.<br/>

## In-Country Verification

In-country status will be automatically recorded once a week by tapping an ID card at store receipt kiosk terminals. If there is no record for over a month, the individual will be automatically deemed to have left the country, and basic livelihood support payments will cease.<br/>

This mechanism is designed to preserve immigrants' freedom of entry and exit.<br/>

## Identity Registration

Granting resident status to anonymous individuals is the Mayor's authority.<br/>

The verification of the process for residents to become immigrants is conducted by the court.<br/>

If the Mayor is unwilling to grant resident status to anonymous individuals, they can also go to the court to register as residents.<br/>

## Segregated Governance Regions

When the population is too large, many small-scale governance regions can be partitioned. Through court adjudication, a portion of residents and immigrants can be relocated into them, with departure prohibited. They are allowed to leave one day per month, but must return within the day. Individuals who are not banned by a court can freely enter and exit the segregated governance region by presenting their ID cards. The segregated governance region remains a fully functional autonomous zone, complete with hospitals, courts, freedom of speech, and everything else needed.<br/>

## Supplementary Explanation

Citizens retain full citizenship rights even after leaving for another country. They can still vote, receive the basic living security payment, and receive sovereign dividends.<br/>

Immigrants are entitled to the basic living security payment only when they are staying in the Transparent Republic.<br/>
