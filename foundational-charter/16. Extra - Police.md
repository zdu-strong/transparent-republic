# Police

Police are divided into **patrol police** and **court police**.<br/>

Everyone has the right to stop violent crime, theft, and robbery. This right has nothing to do with the police and belongs to every citizen; whether excessive force was used in self-defense should be determined by the court.<br/>

## patrol police

Patrol police are police officers within their jurisdiction responsible for patrolling.<br/>

Patrol police hold only two powers: the authority to demand identification and the authority to initiate prosecution.<br/>

Patrol police have no legal obligation to stop crimes.<br/>

If an official demands or threatens a patrol police to perform duties outside their scope of responsibility, the officer may sue the official. The court will hear the case and may impose a fine or sentence the official to imprisonment.<br/>

For example, if an official demands that a patrol officer assist citizens in stopping a theft, the officer may sue the official.<br/>

### the authority to demand identification

Police may ask people to present their ID without explaining the reason to the people, and people have the obligation to present their ID.<br/>

Every time police ask a citizen to present their ID, they must later add the reason for doing so into the system, so that officials can review it and decide whether to continue employing that police officer.<br/>

### the authority to initiate prosecution

Patrol police have no law-enforcement authority. They may only file a lawsuit with the court, and the court will conduct the trial.<br/>

Example:<br/>

1. Prosecute individuals for littering.<br/>
2. Prosecute individuals for failing to produce identification.<br/>

## court police

Court police are police officers dispatched by the court. They are responsible for carrying out specific orders issued by the court.<br/>
