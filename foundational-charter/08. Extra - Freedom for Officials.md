# Freedom for Officials

## Freedom to govern

Political dissatisfaction should be addressed through dismissal, replacement, public scrutiny, and institutional reform, rather than by turning vague allegations into criminal weapons against former officials.<br/>

Public officials must have the freedom to govern, make decisions, lawfully exercise their authority, and make political mistakes. They should not be punished merely because a succeeding government characterizes their past decisions as corruption or abuse of power.<br/>

Corruption and abuse of power are moral accusations; they do not, by themselves, constitute criminal offenses under the law.<br/>

Embezzlement of public funds is a criminal offense. Any prosecution for embezzlement of public funds must be initiated within three months after the alleged act occurred. Once that period has expired, the courts shall no longer accept or adjudicate such cases.<br/>

Salaries and bonuses paid in accordance with official procedures shall not be classified as corruption, regardless of the amount.<br/>

Any crime committed by a public official in the exercise of official authority or the implementation of public policy must be prosecuted within three months. After the statute of limitations has expired, the courts shall no longer accept or hear such cases.<br/>

This limitation does not apply to bodily harm personally inflicted by a law enforcement officer. Such conduct shall remain subject to trial and adjudication by the courts.<br/>

For example, suppose Police Minister A orders Police Officer B to assault Citizen C. Within three months, Police Minister A may be prosecuted for causing harm to Citizen C. Once the three-month period has expired, Police Minister A may no longer be prosecuted for that act.<br/>

Police Officer B’s assault on Citizen C, however, constitutes direct harm and is not subject to any time limit for prosecution. The court shall determine whether Police Officer B should be exempted from criminal liability.<br/>

## Salary Freedom

Community Supervisors with a voting weight exceeding 1,000 voters, National Commissioners, and District Commissioners: Their salaries and bonuses are adjusted by the national payroll department. Upon approval by the President and the Minister of Finance, the changes take effect after a 15-day delay and are disbursed by the Ministry of Finance.<br/>

The President’s salary and bonuses are determined by the President. Upon approval by the Minister of Finance, they take effect after a 15-day delay and are disbursed by the Ministry of Finance.<br/>

The Mayor’s salary and bonuses are determined by the Mayor. Upon approval by the Minister of Finance, they take effect after a 15-day delay and are disbursed by the Ministry of Finance.<br/>

The salaries and bonuses for all ministers and employees—including those in the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Defense, and Ministry of Police—are determined by the respective ministers. Once the ministers submit their compensation plans to the President or the Mayor for approval, the plans take effect immediately and are disbursed by the relevant minister.<br/>

The overall budget allocations for all departments are proposed by the President. Upon co-approval by the Minister of Finance, they take effect after a 15-day delay, with the Ministry of Finance responsible for disbursing funds to all departments. The Ministry of Finance does not manage the internal funds of other departments.<br/>
