# Freedom for Officials

## Freedom to govern

### Political dissatisfaction

Political dissatisfaction should be addressed through dismissal, replacement, public scrutiny, and institutional reform, rather than by turning vague allegations into criminal weapons against former officials.<br/>

### Statute of limitations

Any crime committed by a public official in the exercise of official authority or the implementation of public policy must be prosecuted within three months. After the statute of limitations has expired, the courts shall no longer accept such cases.<br/>

This limitation does not apply to bodily harm personally inflicted by a law enforcement officer. Such conduct shall remain subject to trial and adjudication by the courts.<br/>

#### Example - harm

For example, suppose Police Minister A orders Police Officer B to assault Citizen C. Within three months, Police Minister A may be prosecuted for causing harm to Citizen C. Once the three-month period has expired, Police Minister A may no longer be prosecuted for that act.<br/>

Police Officer B’s assault on Citizen C, however, constitutes direct harm and is not subject to any time limit for prosecution. The court shall determine whether Police Officer B should be exempted from criminal liability.<br/>

#### Example - road repairs

The district head applied for $200 for road repairs, and both the mayor and the finance minister approved the request.<br/>
The district head embezzled the $200 and officially logged the road repair project as successfully completed.<br/>
Once completed, the project entered a public notice period with a three-month window to file lawsuits. A citizen filed a lawsuit against the district head for failing to pave the road; the court dispatched personnel to verify and record the facts, and then closed the case.<br/>
Project failure is not a crime; the court is solely responsible for accepting lawsuits, verifying facts, recording findings, and closing cases.<br/>
The appointment and removal of the district head do not fall under the court's jurisdiction; they are decided independently by the bureaucratic system.<br/>

### Salaries and bonuses

Salaries and bonuses paid in accordance with official procedures are strictly legal, regardless of the amount.<br/>

### Corruption and abuse of power

Corruption and abuse of power are moral accusations; they do not, by themselves, constitute criminal offenses under the law.<br/>

Corruption refers to companies and individuals transferring money to officials; corruption does not need to be disclosed, nor does it need to be reported.<br/>

### Embezzlement

Any prosecution for embezzlement of public funds must be initiated within three months after the alleged act occurred. Once that period has expired, the courts shall no longer accept such cases.<br/>

The court will accept the lawsuit and proceed to trial only if a single expenditure is at least $20,000, or if multiple expenditures under $20,000 incurred within a single day total $20,000 or more.<br/>

It constitutes embezzlement (or misappropriation of public funds) for officials to convey taxpayer money to any third party (including corporations) through improper or unjustified consideration without authorization.<br/>

## Salary Freedom

The salaries and bonuses of the members of the National Supervisory Council members, all ministers and employees, and the President shall be adjusted by the President. Following approval by the Minister of Finance, the adjustments will take effect after 15 days and be disbursed by the Ministry of Finance.<br/>

The salaries and bonuses of the members of the Governance Region Supervisory Council members, all ministers and employees, and the Mayor shall be adjusted by the Mayor. Following approval by the Minister of Finance, the adjustments will take effect after 15 days and be disbursed by the Ministry of Finance.<br/>
