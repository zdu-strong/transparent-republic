# Tax System

The central government collects taxes centrally and allocates 30% of the tax revenue from each Governance Region to the Governance Regions.<br/>
If a Governance Region deems 30% insufficient, it must submit a detailed budget for review by its finance team, after which additional funds will be allocated.<br/>
Governance Regions have full control over their finances, and the central government cannot interfere.<br/>

## Taxes include commodity consumption tax, commodity profit tax, Fixed asset tax.<br/>

### Commodity consumption tax and commodity profit tax

All goods are subject to a unified commodity profit tax rate and a unified commodity consumption tax rate, while different cost ratios are assigned to different product categories.<br/>

For small businesses, product categories are not distinguished. instead, a fixed cost ratio is assigned based on the type of business.<br/>

Production inputs (such as fixed assets, raw materials, and capital goods) are subject to taxation. The government factors the cost of production inputs into its prescribed fixed cost ratio for goods.<br/>

### Fixed asset tax:<br/>

All fixed assets (such as Land) will be subject to a fixed asset tax on a monthly basis according to a specified ratio.<br/>

The President determines the classification, use, and tax rate for fixed assets.<br/>

The Mayor determines the applicable asset classification and its taxable value.<br/>

The taxable value is independent of the actual transaction price.<br/>

It is also completely fine for the mayor to set the taxable value of fixed assets to 0 dollars.<br/>

## Land:<br/>

Land is subject to fixed asset tax, and hotel bookings are taxed as commodity. Buildings, land transactions, and rent are exempt from taxation.<br/>

Buildings cannot be transacted separately and may only be leased.<br/>

When land is transacted, any buildings on it are transferred together with the land.<br/>

The government of the governed region authority zones the land and determines its classification and taxable value. The government, individuals, and companies may only use land according to the permitted use of its land type.<br/>

Land is subject to fixed asset tax, while government-owned land is tax-exempt. Ownership of government-held land may only be transferred through a public auction, with a reserve price of $0, an auction duration of three days, and all auction proceeds reverting to the central government.<br/>

When land belongs to the government, the government can adjust it at its discretion.<br/>

When land is privately owned, the government can only adjust the taxable value, and each time the taxable value is adjusted, the government will provide a purchase contract, leaving it to the landowner to decide whether to sell.<br/>

When a landowner is unable to pay the fixed asset tax, the land will be auctioned. The tenants’ lease contracts will become invalid. The proceeds from the auction will first be used to compensate long-term tenants for their rent, and the remaining amount will be returned to the landowner.<br/>

## Virtual goods:<br/>
Virtual goods are tax-exempt, but physical gifts must first be registered with the government to determine the value of the goods and the applicable tax amount.<br/>
Because no method has been devised to determine a consumer's location without infringing on freedom, virtual goods are temporarily exempt from tax.<br/>


## Contracted Eggs Commodity Category Tax Example:<br/>
  + Cost of Eggs: $0.8
  + Selling Price of Contracted Eggs: $1
  + Fixed Cost Ratio of Contracted Eggs: 80%
  + Commodity Consumption Tax Rate: 10%
  + Commodity Profit Tax Rate: 40%
  + Commodity Consumption Tax Amount: (1 * 0.1 / ( 1 + 0.1 )) = $0.09
  + Commodity Profit Tax Amount: (1 * 0.2 * 0.4 - 0.09) = (0.08 - 0.09) = $0
  + Profit Amount for Contracted Eggs: (1 - 0.09 - 0.8) = $0.11
